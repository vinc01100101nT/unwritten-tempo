# Playstyles & Builds

*Unwritten has **no fixed classes.** You are what you wield and where you invest.*

## Two levers, not a class

There are no Swordsmen or Mages to pick from. Instead, your gameplay identity emerges from two things you fully control:

1. **Your equipped weapon** — it grants your active skill kit.
2. **Your ring build** — where you pour your stat growth.

Change either and your character plays differently. Nothing boxes you into a preset template.

## Your weapon = your abilities

The weapon in your hand decides which **active skills** fill your hotbar. Equip a staff and you cast its spells; pick up a bow and you gain its shots. **Swap the weapon, swap the whole kit.** The weapon's type (sword, staff, bow, dagger…) also sets your attack feel and animation — but it does **not** gate what you're allowed to be. See **Weapons & Equipment** and **Skills**.

## Your ring = your role

Your power scales off your **ring stats**, and that scaling is role-bound: **healing grows with MP**, **damage grows with Attack / Magic Attack**. Because ring investment is slow to change, it's what truly commits you to a role — a healer is someone who poured into MP, a damage-dealer someone who poured into Attack. See **Stats & the Ring**.

## Your playstyle = weapon × ring

Put the two together and you get your character:

- The **weapon** is the fast, swappable half — change your abilities on the fly.
- The **ring** is the slow, committed half — your accumulated power and role.

A staff in the hands of an MP build heals and casts; the same staff on an Attack build barely tickles. That interplay is the whole build system.

## Example playstyles *(emergent, not presets)*

- **Front-line bruiser** — a melee weapon + a STR/VIT ring
- **Ranged damage** — a bow + an Attack/DEX ring
- **Battle-mage** — a staff + a Magic Attack ring
- **Healer / support** — a support weapon + an MP ring

These are just illustrations of what the two levers can produce — you can rebuild toward any of them by changing your weapon and steering your ring; none of them is a locked class.

> *This replaces an earlier "classes" concept that is no longer part of the design. Identity in Unwritten is weapon + ring, full stop.*
