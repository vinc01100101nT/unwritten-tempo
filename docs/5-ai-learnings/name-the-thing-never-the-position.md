# The reader keeps asking "which one?"

## Reach for this when

Somebody has come back twice on the same explanation asking what a word referred to — "which one", "what copy",
"what side" — and each answer needed another answer.

## The rule

**Every noun in an explanation is a NAME or a NUMBER, never a position.** Banned outright: *one side*, *the other
one*, *the second copy*, *which one*, *it*, *there*, *that place*, *the first*. They are all instructions to the
reader to work out what you meant from context they do not have.

Where a thing has no name, **give it one in the sentence and keep using it**: "world server 2" and "world server
3", "your laptop" and "your desktop", "the sword". A made-up label used consistently is infinitely clearer than a
correct word the reader has to resolve.

## Why

2026-09-05, across one long session. The same explanation was rewritten **four times** because of one word each
time:

- *"the saved times are held to one side"* — "what is that one side"
- *"each map has a coordinate"* — "what the actual is a coordinate"
- *"a second copy asks to enter"* — "second copy, what copy"
- *"it does not write down which one"* — "which one"

Every one of those sentences was factually correct. Every one cost a full round, and the user's patience with
it, before the real answer landed. **Four rounds spent on vocabulary, none on the work.**

The version that finally worked named things and numbered them: *"world server 2 is running your character…
world server 3 is handed it… in window A you drop the sword…"*. Nothing about the content changed.

## How to apply

1. **Before sending, read every noun.** For each one ask: *could the reader point at this?* "World server 2" —
   yes. "One side" — no. "The second copy" — no.
2. **Number the actors and keep the numbers.** Two of a thing means "2" and "3", not "one" and "the other".
3. **Walk it as numbered steps whenever two machines are involved.** Prose hides which actor is doing what;
   a numbered list cannot.
4. **A made-up name beats an accurate description.** "The waiting list on your character" beats "one side" even
   though nothing is called that.
5. **When corrected, do not gloss the word — replace the whole explanation.** Adding "by which I mean…" leaves
   the unclear sentence sitting there and makes the reader read it twice.
