# A Shared Rule Change Moves Both Sides of the Fight

**Reach for this when:** you are about to show that changing a rule fixes a lopsided fight, and the rule is one both
fighters use — what gear gives, a stat formula, a speed.

## The rule

Recompute BOTH fighters under the new rule before quoting any "after" number. A rule both sides read moves both
sides, so the gap you set out to close is often unchanged, or wider. Then check where the gap really comes from: if it
is that one side simply has more of the thing (more pieces, more folds, a higher grade), no change to what the thing
is worth can close it.

## Why

On 2026-10-14 a new character could not kill a level-4 creature wearing five pieces of F +8 gear. The proposal was to
make a fold worth a percentage instead of a fixed amount, and the worked example showed the creature falling from 217
health to 60 and dying in about 7 hits. The example had lowered the creature and left the character on its old
numbers. Redone for both, the character did 1 to 7 a hit and died in two — worse than before. The user caught it in
one line: "the player will be affected and will get lowered as well. the newbie problem will remain." Two rounds of
proposal were spent on a fix that could never have worked, because the real gap was three pieces at Raw +5 against
five pieces at F +8.

## How to apply

- Write the before and after as one table with a row per fighter, and fill every cell from the new rule. An "after"
  column with only one fighter in it is the mistake.
- Before proposing a change to what something is worth, count how much of it each side has. If the counts differ, say
  that first: the fix is in who gets how much, not in the worth.
- Measure the fight at the edge that prompted it (the weakest new character against the strongest creature of the
  first field), not at a comfortable middle.
