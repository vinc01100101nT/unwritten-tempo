# A Measured Difference May Not Show On Screen

**Reach for this when** you are about to tell the user why one picture looks better than another.

## The rule

A difference you measured in the data is only the cause of a look if it survives to the pixels. Before naming it as
the cause, check whether it can show at all (sizes against spacing, camera distance), or render both and compare.
Otherwise say "this differs; not yet shown to be what you see".

## Why

2026-10-27: asked why the nature pack's video meadow looked better than ours, I measured that the demo's ground leaves
22% of its spots empty and ours left none, and called that the cause. It was built, and the picture barely changed:
each meadow plant is 0.66–0.88 m wide against 0.29 m spots, so about eight plants cover every point either way. The
real visible differences (one colour per demo patch against our per-plant colour mix, a ground-level camera) came out
only after the build. One round spent on a cause that could not show.

## How to apply

1. For any "this is why it looks X" claim, ask: at the size things are drawn, can this difference reach the screen?
   (Plant width ÷ spacing, distance ÷ camera zoom, a colour change below a few percent.)
2. If unsure, render a private picture with the change faked in before proposing the build.
3. Name every other measured difference beside it, so the user is not handed one cause as the whole answer.
