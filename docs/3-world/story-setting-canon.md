# Unwritten — Story & Setting (Canon)

The anchor lore doc. Everything else (world, mobs, weapons, progression visuals) should be consistent with this. The core discovery: **the game's visual split — flat 2D pages vs. 3D charcoal people — IS the story.** Nothing about the art is arbitrary.

## The premise

An artist wrote a story in a book and drew its pictures himself. The story was gentle, pure and peaceful. **Everything he finished is on the page, drawn flat** — the 2D characters. **Everyone still only in his imagination is unwritten** — real in his mind, not yet on the page: every 3D person, the Kashira, and you.

The artist was troubled by unwanted thoughts that kept breaking in, and the pictures in his head grew chaotic. The story gives those thoughts one shape and one name: **Chaos**. Chaos lives only inside the artist's mind. It has tried again and again to wreck his peace and his book, and he has fought back every time — the battles all happen inside his mind.

To fight it he imagined **fourteen champions, the Fourteen Kashira**. Together they struck Chaos down and sealed it away — or believed they had — but not before it **corrupted pages of the book**. Those corrupted pages are the 2D mobs.

**The seal closed on an empty shell.** In the battle's last moments Chaos slipped into one of the Fourteen — **Veyra**, the Sword — and walked home among them wearing her face. **Morwen**, the Scythe, saw it and said so. Veyra accused her back, and most of the Fourteen believed the hero over the curse-caster. The artist heard both and could not tell which of his champions was lying, so he doubted every one of them until, in his mind, his fear turned all fourteen — **the fourteen shadows**. Thirteen of them are innocent. Chaos lives in one.

Now only the unwritten are on the artist's side. **Your goal is to end the paranoia**: restore the corrupted pages, remember the Fourteen one by one until each is proven, and in the end face the shadow where Chaos truly lives. The full battle and what came after it are in **The Fourteen Kashira** below.

- **Chaos corrupts; it never erases.** It blots, smears and twists what is written. "Unwritten" only ever means *not yet written* — never *undone*.
- **The artist is never named and never appears.**
- **Care rule:** Chaos is unwanted thoughts and paranoia, never a named real diagnosis. The story is about the artist fighting back and getting better. His fear was right about one champion; **what he did wrong was condemning all fourteen without proof.** Getting better is judging each one on evidence instead of fearing everyone — and that evidence can only come from outside his mind, from the unwritten. Every later beat must keep this framing.

## Why the art means what it means

- **2D flat mobs = corrupted pages.** They are literally paper, and Chaos is what twisted them.
- **The 2D characters = those pages as the artist drew them.** Beating a mob can turn it back into its true character — see [Saved Characters](saved-characters.md).
- **3D-but-charcoal players and NPCs = the unwritten.** They exist in the artist's mind but have not been put on the page, so no colour has reached them. The charcoal body + coloured hair + ink-eye rig telegraphs exactly this: a trace of identity waiting to be written in.
- **Beating a mob = restoring a page**, and what it drops is what that page gives back.
- **"Unwritten"** = everyone still in the artist's imagination — including you.

## The Fourteen Kashira

The artist imagined fourteen champions to fight Chaos. He gave every one of them a **name**, but never wrote a name
on the page, so they are still unwritten: their names are spoken, never written. They struck Chaos down, the seal
closed on an empty shell, and his paranoia turned all fourteen into the fourteen shadows.

- **Each of the 14 weapons is one of them.** A weapon is what the artist kept of a Kashira from before they fell. The
  one-sentence identity in `active-skill-system-v3/weapon-roster-12-identities.md` **is** that Kashira's character —
  Asterion is the one who stood in front, Velmora the one nobody saw arrive.
- **Each has a name and a title** — *Veyra, the Last Petal* — and is also called by their **style**: the Blossom Wind
  Kashira. Their shadows bear the same name and title.
- **Learning a weapon is remembering who its Kashira really was, as they were before they fell.** A weapon holds all
  of one Kashira, and what you can draw out of it is however much of them you have lived: each skill can come back
  once your mastery of that weapon reaches it, and you choose which to remember. A finer blade lets you remember
  further. Two people carrying the same kind of weapon know different halves of the same Kashira.
- **Every Kashira remembered frees the artist a little.** Knowing who they truly were proves to him that Chaos never
  had them. For the Sword it proves something harder: remembering the real Veyra proves that the shadow at level 170
  is Chaos wearing her face, not her. A player masters whichever weapons they choose — nobody has to learn all
  fourteen.
- **Carrying one bleeds their identity into you** — that is the charcoal → colour return. You are not recovering *the*
  truth; you are finishing *a* story.
- **Writing a Kashira's name back into the book is the mastery endgame.** Learn enough of a weapon's pool and its
  Kashira's name can finally be put on the page — a codex entry authored by play. Fourteen names to write, fourteen
  mastery tracks.

### The legends

One line per Kashira, in the order of the battle. Read top to bottom, they are the battle in brief; the full telling
is **The whole story** below.

1. **Celestia** — When Chaos first came, Celestia drifted above it like a cloud nobody could catch, and rained arrows
   on it until it turned to chase her.
2. **Orion** — It chased her straight into Orion, who caught it the way a river catches a falling branch, and bent
   its path to where the others waited.
3. **Mordane** — There Mordane rose like a raging tide, and one swing swept its whole front off the road.
4. **Tharos** — What survived lunged at Orion, until Tharos leapt and brought his hammer down hard enough to break a
   falling star. The earth split open beneath it, and the whole field froze mid-breath.
5. **Asterion** — Asterion stepped in front of the others, took every blow meant for them, then spent everything he
   had on one fist that lit the sky like the northern lights.
6. **Varkhaz** — Through that light Varkhaz charged, never once stepping back, each blow catching the next like
   wildfire in dry grass.
7. **Veyra** — Veyra dashed past him into the very heart of it and cut it a thousand times in a few heartbeats. The
   heart died, and as it died Chaos fled into the one who had killed it. Veyra fell before the petals settled.
8. **Seraphine** — Seraphine ran to her through the storm. As the morning mist settled over her, Veyra opened her eyes
   and stood, and Seraphine felt something cold look back at her. She said nothing.
9. **Riven** — The dark, empty now but still moving, split into a hundred shapes, and Riven answered with a hundred
   blades, whirling through them like leaves in an autumn gale.
10. **Velmora** — In the confusion nobody saw Velmora arrive. She nicked it once with a blade dark with nightshade,
    and stepped back to let the poison bloom.
11. **Kaelen** — As it seemed to weaken, Kaelen set the ground beneath it burning, then froze the fire to ash under
    ice, so it had nowhere left to stand.
12. **Morwen** — Only Morwen saw what the others missed: her curse on the dark never took hold, because nothing was
    inside it, and Veyra's petals had stopped falling. Telling no one yet, she laid a second, slower curse on Veyra.
13. **Nysara** — Nysara circled unseen, a mirage in the dust, until one crack showed, then struck that same spot again
    and again until it split wide.
14. **Vael** — For three moonless nights Vael had lain still on the farthest hill. Through Nysara's crack he fired one
    shot out of the dark, and the seal closed. Fourteen walked home. But the seal had closed on an empty shell, and
    Chaos walked home with them, wearing Veyra's face.

### The whole story

**Before the battle.** For three moonless nights before anyone else moved, Vael lay on the farthest hill above the
field with his rifle across his knees, watching the dark. He had told the others he would fire once, and only when
there was something worth hitting. Nobody argued. Vael had never missed, mostly because he had never hurried.

**The opening.** Chaos came the way bad thoughts come — all at once and from every side. Celestia went first, because
somebody had to make it choose a direction. She drifted above it like a passing cloud, never where it reached for her,
and rained arrows into it until it lost patience and turned to chase her across the plain.

**The river.** She led it straight to Orion. He had chosen the ground days earlier: a bend where the land narrowed, the
one place the others could meet it together. When it came pouring after Celestia he hooked it with his spear and
dragged it, step by step, the way a river bends whatever falls into it, until it stood exactly where he wanted it.

**The tide and the earth.** Mordane was waiting there. He rose like a raging tide, and one sweep of his greatsword
crashed over its front and cleared the road. What survived came back at Orion with everything it had — until Tharos
leapt from the ridge and brought his hammer down hard enough, the others swore, to break a falling star. The earth
split open under it, and for one breath the whole field stood frozen.

**The light.** Chaos found its feet and threw itself at the others. Asterion stepped in front of all of them. He took
every blow that was meant for someone else — blocked them, turned them back, refused to fall — and when it finally
staggered, he gathered every scrap of strength he had left into a single fist. When it landed, the sky above the
field lit green and violet, like the northern lights.

**The charge.** Through that light came Varkhaz. He did not defend and he did not step back. Each blow of his axe
caught the next, the way wildfire runs through dry grass, until the dark ahead of him was nothing but a narrowing
corridor — and at the end of it, the heart.

**The thousand cuts.** Veyra was already moving. She dashed past Varkhaz down that corridor, petals streaming behind
her, and cut the heart of Chaos a thousand times in a few heartbeats. Veyra had never lost an exchange, and she did not
lose this one. The heart died. But as it died, Chaos did what it had planned from the start: it left the heart and fled
into the one who had killed it. Veyra fell before the petals settled.

**The dew.** Seraphine reached her through the storm and knelt beside her. Bringing the fallen back was what Seraphine
was for — she had done it a hundred times that night. As the morning mist gathered around them, Veyra opened her eyes
and stood. And Seraphine, still holding her hand, felt something cold look back at her out of Veyra's face. She told
herself she was tired. She said nothing.

**The empty dark.** Behind them, the dark kept fighting. Nothing was inside it now, but Chaos left it moving, because a
battle that suddenly stopped would have given everything away. It split into a hundred shapes to hold the Fourteen's
attention, and Riven answered with a hundred blades, flung and whirling through every one of them like leaves in an
autumn gale.

**The poison and the ash.** In the confusion nobody saw Velmora arrive. She nicked the dark once with a blade wet with
nightshade and stepped back to let the poison bloom. It seemed to weaken. Kaelen set the ground beneath it burning,
then froze the flames to ash under ice, so it had nowhere left to stand.

**The lily.** Morwen did nothing quickly. She laid one quiet curse on the dark, as she always did, and waited for it to
take hold. It never did. A curse cannot take hold of something with nothing inside it — and Morwen understood what that
meant. She looked across the field at Veyra, standing again, and noticed what no one else had: wherever Veyra
moved, petals had always fallen. Now they had stopped. Telling no one yet, Morwen laid a second curse, a slow one, on
Veyra herself.

**The crack.** Nysara had been circling the whole time, unseen, a mirage in the dust. When the poison and the ash had
done their work, one crack finally showed in the dark. She struck that exact spot, again and again and again, until
it split wide.

**The shot.** On the farthest hill, three nights of waiting ended. Through Nysara's crack Vael fired his one shot out
of the dark, and it fell like a star. The seal closed. The battle was over. Fourteen walked home.

**After.** Morwen did not stay quiet. She stood in front of the others and said that Chaos had not been sealed — that it
was here, among them, in Veyra. But her curse had not bloomed yet, and she had nothing to show them. Veyra did not
deny it angrily. She turned it around, gently: the curse-caster who sees rot everywhere, accusing the one who had just
died for them. Was it not more likely that Chaos had found its way into Morwen? Seraphine knew the truth, but saying
it would mean admitting she had brought Chaos home with her own hands, so she stood beside Veyra. One by one, most of
the others followed her.

**The fear.** The artist heard all of it. He could not tell which of his champions was lying, because Chaos is made of
his own thoughts, and a thought wearing the face of his own creation feels exactly like his own. Two of his champions
accused each other and twelve took sides, any of whom could be fooled, or lying. His doubt did not stop at the two. It
reached every one of them, until, in his mind, his fear turned all fourteen into shadows. Chaos never needed to fight
him again. Turning him against his own champions had been the attack all along.

**The mark.** Days later, Morwen's slow curse finally bloomed: a black lily mark on Veyra. But by then there was no
Veyra left to show it to — only her shadow. Nobody had ever seen the mark on the Kashira, and the mark is still there
on her shadow now. It is the proof. Morwen did the right thing and caused the tragedy anyway, and her shadow is the
most bitter of the fourteen.

**Now.** Thirteen of the shadows are innocent: they are the artist's fear wearing his champions' shapes, and they come
back whenever the fear does. The fourteenth is Chaos wearing Veyra's face. The artist cannot sort them out himself.
The proof has to come from outside his mind — from the unwritten, remembering each Kashira as they really were, one
at a time, on evidence instead of fear.

### What the shadows are

- **Veyra's shadow holds Chaos**, and carries Morwen's black lily mark. It is always the level-170 shadow, whatever the
  other thirteen's order.
- **The other thirteen are the artist's fear in their shape**, not the Kashira themselves. Beating one breaks that
  fear for a while; it comes back because the fear comes back, for every player. Remembering a Kashira is each
  player's own progress, never something that removes a shadow from the world.

**Their styles.** Each Kashira fought in a **style** — how they fought, which is all anyone could carry away. Every
skill of a weapon is one numbered **form** of its style, counted in the order the weapon teaches it (the Sword's first
form is Piercing Gust), and a form's name is built only from its own style's picture — Blossom Wind's forms are wind,
petals, branches and storm; Autumn Gale's are leaves.
**Every style is a picture of nature** — a living thing, a natural scene, or the weather or season that moves it, in
one, two or three words: Wildfire, Blossom Wind, Bending River, Black Lily. Never a material or a machine (no iron,
no gate, no stone wall). It is real English, spelled as English spells it, sounds smooth said with "style", never
names a real country's culture and never borrows another story's term. The one word outside this rule is their rank,
**Kashira** (Japanese for a head, a chief), chosen by the user on 2026-09-30.

**Calling a form.** Every skill is called out over the caster's head and in the chat log each time it fires, with no
wait between calls: by its name alone ("Last Petal!!"), and a passive only when it is efficient. An **efficient** cast —
5% of the casts of any trademark skill, whatever weapon is held, ultimates included, raising that cast's numbers by a
further +20% —
makes the full call ("Blossom Wind style, 21st form, Thousand Petals!!"). People, the Kashira and their shadows make
the full call; an ordinary creature only names the form. The server rolls the efficiency and chooses the words; other
players' calls can be hidden in the settings.

*The names, titles, genders and styles are LOCKED (2026-10-06). Form names are working names and can still change.*

## Mechanical hooks this justifies (design directions, not commitments)

- **Charcoal → colour as progression.** As a player is written in (levels, ring points, story beats, weapon mastery), colour returns to them — identity arriving for the first time. The rig already supports a gradient from charcoal toward a fully coloured self.
- **Beating a mob = restoring a page.** A restored page can turn back into its true character — [Saved Characters](saved-characters.md). Drops can be *fragments* that assemble into written lore entries — a codex the player authored.
- **Ring of Progression = a vessel that holds written identity.** Tradeable, with lineage; the conservation economy fits — what's written is *passed on, never minted*. See the **Ring System — Current Facts (Trio-Ring)** doc (the tradeable-ring direction is recorded there as a proposed, uncommitted concept).
- **One shadow holds Chaos.** Veyra's shadow is where Chaos lives; the other thirteen are the artist's fear in their shape. Ending the paranoia means proving the thirteen innocent and facing the one.
- **Conservation as balance driver.** Every gain pays a cost — mirrors "a line written here is a line not written elsewhere."
- **Weapon mastery = writing a Kashira's name back into the book.** Per-weapon codex track; see **The Fourteen Kashira** above and the weapon roster doc.
