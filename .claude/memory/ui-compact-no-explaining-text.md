On 2026-10-11 the user rejected the Character window and the bag as "too oversized… the white space is so huge", and then rejected my first compact redesign's wording: "remove unnecessary texts like 'no points to spend, level up to earn one', players know that… and 'Your ring', poor design". They also had "next +4 at 10" removed from the stat rows ("it is not needed"). What they approved: the ring drawn as an actual circle whose stats are clicked directly, the leader always at the top, spinning smoothly to a new pick.

**Why:** their words — players already know; no need to be explicit. Empty-state sentences, section headings over things that are self-evident, and instructional hints read as poor design to them.

**How to apply:** when designing or prototyping any in-game window — size it to its content, small rows and small type; write no empty-state message (show nothing), no heading that names what is plainly there, no "choose one…" instruction; prefer making the drawn thing itself clickable over a separate row of buttons plus a list. Offer two or three distinct layouts side by side when asked to rethink. Wording: the Ring of Progression's points are **"ring points"** — "skill points" reads as mastery points in the Skills window. (The "prefer clickable drawing" generalisation is my reading of one approval, not something they stated as a rule.)
