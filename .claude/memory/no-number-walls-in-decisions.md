On 2026-10-13, relaying a planner's toughness formula, I sent three tables of percentages, three "findings" and three decisions in one reply. The user: "your tables are nightmare to digest, confusing bullshit. absolute bullshit."

**Why:** the user does not want to audit intermediate numbers or weigh options I am able to weigh myself; a wall of figures hides the one thing they need to know. (My interpretation: the earlier replies in the same thread — a cap explained with two tables, then a third — had already been confusing, so this was the third strike, not the first.)

**How to apply:** for a design result, write what it means for the game in two or three plain sentences with at most one or two example numbers. Decide the open points myself, list each call in one line, and proceed; ask only if a call is truly the user's, and then one question. A table only when the user asks to compare rows. Sub-agent tables stay in my context — relay the conclusion, not the grid. Related: [[dont-agree-reflexively]], [[just-do-it-dont-instruct]].
